# Abbreviations in written German (Abkürzungen)

> 🇩🇪 Deutsch: [`../../de/referenz/abkuerzungen.md`](../../de/referenz/abkuerzungen.md)

These show up constantly in texts, textbooks and on Lingolia. In speech they are
usually said in full.

## Quick reference

| Abbr. | written out | meaning | spoken? |
|------|----------------|----------------|-------------|
| z. B. | zum Beispiel | for example | yes, said in full |
| bzw. | beziehungsweise | or rather / respectively / or | yes, in full |
| d. h. | das heißt | that is / i.e. | yes, in full |
| u. a. | unter anderem / und andere | among others | yes, in full |
| usw. | und so weiter | and so on / etc. | yes, in full |
| etc. | et cetera | etc. | "et cetera" |
| ca. | circa | approximately | "circa" |
| vgl. | vergleiche | compare / cf. | rarely spoken |
| bzgl. | bezüglich | regarding | usually "bezüglich" |
| ggf. | gegebenenfalls | if applicable / if necessary | in full |
| i. d. R. | in der Regel | as a rule / usually | in full |
| o. Ä. | oder Ähnliches | or similar | in full |
| s. o. / s. u. | siehe oben / siehe unten | see above / see below | in full |
| Nr. | Nummer | number | "Nummer" |
| Abs. | Absatz | paragraph | "Absatz" |
| Str. | Straße | street | "Straße" |

> Spelling: multi-part abbreviations take a **narrow space** ("z. B.", "d. h."). In
> everyday writing many people write "z.B." with no space — you'll meet both.

## bzw. — beziehungsweise

Very common in written German, far more so than its English equivalents.
Three uses:

| Use | Closest English | Example |
|-----|-----------------|---------|
| refining what you just said | *or rather* · *or more precisely* | *…ihrer Rolle im Satz **bzw.** abhängig von Verben…* |
| pairing two lists item by item | *X … and Y respectively* (sometimes just *and* / *or*) | *Anna und Ben → Berlin **bzw.** München* |
| offering another option | *or* · *and/or* | *per Post **bzw.** E-Mail* |

These are not three separate words but **one** connector: "the correspondingly fitting
element for the other one." The English column is only *where it usually lands* — pick
the wording that makes your sentence natural, and don't translate *bzw.* one-to-one.

### 1. "more precisely / rather" (or rather) — narrowing the previous statement
- *Ich komme morgen, bzw. eigentlich erst übermittag.*
  → I'm coming tomorrow — or rather, not until midday.
- *Wir treffen uns im Büro bzw. in dem, was davon übrig ist.*

### 2. "respectively" — mapping two lists onto each other
- *Anna und Ben fahren nach Berlin bzw. München.*
  → Anna goes to Berlin, Ben to Munich.
- *Die Kurse A1 und A2 dauern drei bzw. vier Monate.*
  → A1 lasts three months, A2 four.

### 3. "or / and/or" — naming an alternative (most common, weakest meaning)
- *Bitte schicken Sie das Formular per Post bzw. per E-Mail.*
- *der Ehepartner bzw. die Ehepartnerin* → the spouse (m or f)

### Important: *bzw.* is neither "or" nor "and"
English has no single word for it. Depending on phrasing, *bzw.* becomes **or**, **and**,
**respectively**, or nothing at all (sentence restructured).
*bzw.* really means "correspondingly / the fitting one for the other element".

The same German statement, two English routes:
- *Anna und Ben fahren nach Berlin bzw. München.*
  → "Anna and Ben go to Berlin **and** Munich respectively." **or**
    "Anna goes to Berlin, Ben to Munich." (no word, restructured)
- *Die Fenster sind 1 m bzw. 1,5 m breit.* → "…1 m **and** 1.5 m wide" / "…**or**…"
- *per Post bzw. E-Mail* → "by post **or** email"

Takeaway: don't translate word for word. Read *bzw.* as "…and the correspondingly
fitting one for the other", then pick the English word that makes the sentence natural.

### Further notes
- Comma before *bzw.*: usually **omitted** (uses 2 and 3), often set for use 1.
- Register: neutral to formal. Fine in essays, emails, official texts.
- In casual speech people just say *oder* or the full form *beziehungsweise*.

## d. h. — das heißt

Introduces an **explanation / clarification** (i.e., in other words). A comma follows.
- *Er ist Frühaufsteher, d. h., er steht meistens um fünf Uhr auf.*
- Difference from *z. B.*: *d. h.* restates **the same thing**, *z. B.* gives **one
  example** out of several.

## z. B. — zum Beispiel

Gives an example. No comma needed after it unless the sentence structure requires one.
- *Obst wie z. B. Äpfel und Birnen.*
- *Man kann viel machen, z. B. ins Kino gehen.*
